Reciprocity is largely preserved under the missed synapse model because removing a few synapses usually weakens existing connections rather than deleting them completely, so most bidirectional neuron pairs remain intact. In the false synapse model, reciprocity shows a small initial decrease because only a few randomly sampled one-way connections are added. As the error rate increases, more biologically plausible connections are introduced between neurons with similar circuit neighborhoods, creating additional bidirectional pairs and resulting in an overall increase in reciprocity.

# 1. In-degree KS Distance

### What does it measure?

The KS (Kolmogorov–Smirnov) distance measures **how different the entire in-degree distribution becomes compared to the original network**. A larger value means the network's connectivity pattern has changed more.

### Presentation paragraph

> **"This graph shows how the in-degree distribution changes with increasing error. The missed synapse model causes only a very small increase in KS distance, indicating that removing synapses has little effect on the overall degree distribution. In contrast, the false synapse model produces a much larger increase because adding new connections changes how incoming connections are distributed across neurons. At 20% error, the KS distance is about fifteen times larger than for the missed synapse model, showing that false synapses distort the network structure much more strongly."**

---

# 2. In-degree Wasserstein Distance

### What does it measure?

The Wasserstein distance measures **how much the average in-degree distribution shifts** compared to the original graph. Unlike KS, which focuses on the largest difference between distributions, Wasserstein measures the **overall amount of change**.

### Presentation paragraph

> **"The Wasserstein distance shows the overall shift in the in-degree distribution. Similar to the KS distance, the missed synapse model causes only a gradual increase because most existing connections remain intact. However, the false synapse model produces a much larger increase, as every added edge introduces additional incoming connections and progressively shifts the degree distribution away from the original network."**

---

# 3. PageRank – Overlap of Top-Ranked Neurons

### What does it measure?

This graph measures **how many of the highest-ranked hub neurons remain the same after introducing errors**.

* **1.0** means exactly the same top neurons.
* **Lower values** mean the hub ranking has changed.

### Presentation paragraph

> **"This graph shows how well the most important hub neurons are preserved. The missed synapse model maintains a very high overlap, remaining around 97% even at the highest error rate, indicating that the same neurons continue to act as network hubs. In contrast, the false synapse model steadily reduces the overlap to about 89% at 20% error because adding new connections changes the relative importance of neurons and alters the PageRank-based hub ranking."**

---

## Overall takeaway for this slide

> **"Overall, these results show that missed synapses mainly weaken existing connections while preserving the network's overall structure. In contrast, false synapses introduce new connections that significantly alter the degree distribution and change which neurons act as the network's most important hubs."**
